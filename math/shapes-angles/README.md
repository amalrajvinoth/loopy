# Shapes and Angles

A geometry game with drawings, for ages 7 to 14. The child sees a shape, two lines, a grid or an angle. One question goes with it. The child picks the answer. After each answer, one line says why. In German the game is called "Formen und Winkel".

[Play it](https://amalrajvinoth.github.io/loopy/math/shapes-angles/)

## How to play

1. Look at the drawing and the question under it, like "Which shape is this?" or "What is the area?".
2. Tap the answer you think is right. The right one turns green.
3. One line says why, like "A pentagon has 5 sides and 5 corners." After a wrong answer it says the right answer first.
4. Tap **Next** to go on. Every right answer earns a star.

Level 1 picks from 3 answers. The other levels pick from 4. Yes or no questions have 2.

On a keyboard, press 1 to 4 to pick an answer and **Enter** to go on.

## Age levels

| Age | Level | What comes up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 to 8 | Shapes, corners and sides | Which shape is this? How many corners, how many straight sides? Triangle, square, rectangle, circle, oval, pentagon and hexagon |
| 9 | Lines and symmetry | How many right angles? Are two lines parallel, perpendicular or crossing at a slant? Is the dashed line a line of symmetry? How many lines of symmetry? Naming adds octagon, rhombus, trapezium, parallelogram and kite |
| 10 | Perimeter and area | A rectangle on squared paper: count the squares for the area, go round for the perimeter. Then a rectangle with a corner cut out |
| 11 to 12 | Angles | What kind of angle: acute, right, obtuse, straight or reflex? About how big is it, to the nearest 10°? Angles on a straight line make 180°, in a right angle 90° |
| 13 and up | Triangles and formulas | Equilateral, isosceles, scalene or right-angled? The missing angle in a triangle (180°) or a quadrilateral (360°). Area of a triangle, area and perimeter of a rectangle from its side lengths |

At every level, about one question in five comes from the level below, so what the child learned before stays fresh.

The level shows next to the score, like "Level 3 of 5". After 10 right in a row, or 18 of the last 20 right, the game goes up a level by itself. It says so, with a **Back a step** button. After 3 wrong in the last 5 it offers an easier one, once a visit. A level picked by hand in Settings is kept for that age. When the age goes up, a level the child reached is kept, never lost.

## The drawings

Every drawing is made on the device. No pictures are downloaded. Shapes are drawn to scale. A right angle is a right angle, an angle of 120° is 120°, and a rectangle on the grid is really 4 by 3 squares. Equal sides in a triangle get a small mark, and a right angle gets the little square. The colors follow the theme, and on the printed worksheet everything is black on white.

## No repeats

- A question does not come back within 20 questions. Every question has a short code, like `ar.4x3` (area of a 4 by 3 rectangle), and the game remembers the last 20.
- The same kind of question does not come 3 times in a row while another kind is possible.
- A missed question still comes back on purpose, as Try again, until it is answered right twice. It waits while the child is on another level.

## Last 5

At the top, **Last 5** shows your last 5 answers as dots: green for right, red for wrong. The newest one is on the right.
They stay there next time you play. **Reset score** in Settings clears them.

## Settings

Open them with the gear button.

- **Level.** The five levels above. The game picks one from the age. A changed level is remembered for that age.
- **Read aloud.** A speaker button on the card reads the question and the answers out loud, like "Which shape is this? Triangle, square or pentagon?". It is on by itself for ages 5 to 7.
- Age, colors, sound effects and language (EN/DE) are shared with the other games.
- **Print worksheet.** See below.

## Print a worksheet

Tap **Settings**, then **Print worksheet**. The print window opens. Print it, or save it as a PDF.

- Page 1 has 20 drawings at the game's level, each with its question and the answers to circle.
- Page 2 has the answers.

## Grown-ups' page

Each answer is logged with its topic. So the grown-ups' page can say "Perimeter and area: missed 3 times this week", and **Practise** opens the game on that level. Right answers count by topic too. The five topics are shapes, corners and sides; right angles, lines and symmetry; perimeter and area; angles; triangles and formulas.

## Example help text

- "A hexagon has 6 sides and 6 corners, like a honeycomb cell."
- "Fold a rectangle along its diagonal and the corners stick out. The halves do not match, so the diagonal is not a line of symmetry."
- "3 rows of 4 squares: 4 × 3 = 12 cm²."
- "This angle is 120°. Between 90° and 180° is obtuse."
- "The 3 angles of a triangle add up to 180°. 180 − 50 − 60 = 70."
