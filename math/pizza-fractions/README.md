# Pizza Fractions

See a pizza or a bar cut into equal pieces, and find the fraction.
Then compare two fractions, and add halves and quarters.
The picture shows why, so fractions make sense.

[Play it](https://amalrajvinoth.github.io/loopy/math/pizza-fractions/)

## How to play

There are three kinds of problems:

1. **Name it.** A pizza has some slices with toppings, or a bar has some pieces colored in. Tap the fraction that shows that part, like 3/4.
2. **Compare.** Two fractions, like 3/4 and 5/8. Tap **<**, **=** or **>**.
3. **Add.** Two fractions, like 1/2 + 1/4. Tap the answer.

After each answer the game shows the picture and a short reason. Tap **Next** to go on.

You can also use a keyboard. **1** to **4** picks an answer (or **<**, **=** and **>** when comparing). **Enter** goes on.

## The pictures

- **Pizza:** a round pizza cut into equal slices. Slices with toppings are the colored part.
- **Bar:** a long bar cut into equal pieces, colored in from the left.
- When comparing, the two fractions are drawn side by side (pizzas) or one above the other (bars), in two colors.
- When adding, one picture shows the sum. The first part is one color and the second part is another, cut into the same size pieces.

## Age levels

| Age | What comes up |
| --- | --- |
| 7 and under | Name it, with up to 4 pieces (halves, thirds, quarters) |
| 8 | Name it, with up to 8 pieces |
| 9 | Compare, with up to 8 pieces |
| 10 | Add, with up to 8 pieces |
| 11 and up | All three mixed, with up to 12 pieces |

Adding only uses fractions whose pieces fit together, like halves and quarters, or thirds and sixths. The sum is never more than one whole.
With up to 4 pieces, adding uses only halves and quarters.

## Last 5

At the top, **Last 5** shows your last 5 answers as dots: green for right, red for wrong. The newest one is on the right.
They stay there next time you play. **Reset score** in Settings clears them.

## Settings

Tap **Settings** to choose:

- **Problems:** Name it, Compare, Add, or Mix.
- **Pieces:** up to 4, up to 8, or up to 12 pieces.
- **Picture:** Pizza, Bar, or Both (a pizza or a bar, picked for each problem). Pizza for ages 8 and under, Both from 9.
- **Show the picture before answering:** for Compare and Add. On for ages 8 and under. When it is off, the picture shows after the answer. In Name it the picture is always there, because it is the question.
- **Read aloud:** On or Off. A speaker button sits in the corner of the problem. It says, for example, "Is 3 quarters smaller than, equal to, or bigger than 5 eighths?". It starts on for ages 5 to 7 and off from 8. If the device has no voice, the button and the setting do not show.

Settings are remembered for each age.
The level shows next to the score, like "Level 2 of 5". After 10 right in a row the game offers the next level. After 3 wrong in the last 5 it offers an easier one, once a visit. A level picked by hand in Settings is kept for that age. The levels: name it with 4 pieces, name it with 8, compare with 8, add with 8, all three mixed with 12. The picture settings stay as they are.

## Print a worksheet

Tap **Settings**, then **Print worksheet**. The print window opens. Print it, or save it as a PDF.

- Page 1 has 20 problems at the game's level, in black and white.
- Name it shows a grid of pizzas or bars with a line to write the fraction.
- Compare has a circle to write <, = or > in. Add has a line for the answer.
- Page 2 has the answers.
- Each tap makes a new set of problems.

## Example help text

The game shows a simple reason after each answer. For example:

- "The pizza has 4 equal slices, and 3 have toppings. That is 3 quarters: 3/4."
- "Both are cut into 8 equal pieces. 5 pieces are more than 3, so 5/8 is bigger."
- "Both have 1 piece. Cut into 2, the pieces are bigger than cut into 4. So 1/2 is bigger."
- "1/2 and 2/4 are the same amount, so they are equal. Cut each piece of 1/2 into 2, and you get 2/4."
- "Cut both into 12 pieces: 2/3 is 8/12 and 3/4 is 9/12. 9 is more than 8, so 3/4 is bigger."
- "First make the pieces the same size: 1/2 is 2/4. Then 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4."
- "Don't add the bottom numbers. They tell the size of the pieces, and that stays the same."
