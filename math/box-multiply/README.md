# Box Multiplication

Multiply big numbers with the box method, also called the grid or area method. The German name is "Malkreuz".
Split the numbers into tens and ones, fill one box for each pair, then add the boxes up. For ages 7 to 12.

[Play it](https://amalrajvinoth.github.io/loopy/math/box-multiply/)

## How to play

1. Look at the sum on top, like 23 × 14.
2. The grid is already split for you: 20 and 3 across the top, 10 and 4 down the side.
3. Fill the boxes one at a time. The line under the grid says which one is next, like "Box 3 of 4: 20 × 4 = ?".
4. Tap the numbers, then tap **Check**. A right box turns green and the next one starts.
5. When every box is full, add them up and write the total.
6. Tap **Next** for a new sum.

You can also type on a keyboard. Use the number keys, **Backspace** to erase and **Enter** to check. On the decimals level, the **.** or **,** key writes the decimal point.

## When a box is wrong

- **First try wrong:** the box turns red and a hint shows, like "Work out 2 × 4, then add one zero." Type again.
- **Second try wrong:** the right number goes in the box, with a red border, and the next box starts.

A sum with no mistakes gives a star. A sum with a mistake still gets finished, and it can come back later in **Try again**.

## Steps and Quick

In **Settings**, choose how to play:

- **Steps:** fill every box, then the total. This is how the method is taught.
- **Quick:** the grid is filled in except one box or the total. Find that one number. Good once the method is known.

## Age levels

| Level | Age | What comes up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 7 and under | Two digits times 2, 3, 4 or 5, like 13 × 4 (2 boxes) |
| 2 | 8 | Two digits times one digit, like 47 × 6 (2 boxes) |
| 3 | 9 | Three digits times one digit, like 234 × 7 (3 boxes) |
| 4 | 10 | Two digits times two digits, like 23 × 14 (4 boxes) |
| 5 | 11 | Three digits times two digits, like 123 × 24 (6 boxes) |
| 6 | 12 and up | Decimals, like 2.3 × 14 (4 boxes) |

No digit is ever 0, so every box is used.

The level shows next to the score, like "Level 4 of 6". After 10 right in a row the game offers the next level. After 3 wrong in the last 5 it offers an easier one, once a visit. A level picked by hand in Settings is kept for that age.

## Last 5

At the top, **Last 5** shows your last 5 sums as dots: green for right, red for wrong. The newest one is on the right.
**Reset score** in Settings clears them.

## Settings

Tap **Settings** to choose:

- **How to play:** Steps or Quick.
- **Level:** one of the six levels above.
- **Read aloud:** On or Off. When it is on, a speaker button sits in the corner of the card. It says, for example, "What is 23 times 14?". It starts on for ages 5 to 7 and off from 8. If the device has no voice, the button and the setting do not show.

## Print a worksheet

Tap **Settings**, then **Print worksheet**. The print window opens. Print it, or save it as a PDF.

- Page 1 has 20 sums at the game's level. Each has an empty grid with the parts filled in, and a line for the total.
- Page 2 has the answers.
- Each tap makes a new set of sums.

## Example help text

- A hint for 20 × 4: "Work out 2 × 4, then add one zero."
- A hint for 200 × 40: "Work out 2 × 4, then add 3 zeros."
- A hint for 3 × 4: "Count in 4s, 3 times."
- A hint for 0.3 × 4: "Work out 3 × 4 first, then make it 10 times smaller."
- A hint for the total: "Add up all 4 boxes. Start with the biggest."
- After a second wrong total: "Row by row: 230 + 92. Together 322."
- After the second wrong try: "20 × 4 = 80. On to the next box."
- At the end: "23 × 14 = 322."

## How it differs from the other times games

- **Times and Share** asks only for the answer. This game shows the steps in between.
- **Times Tables Sprint** is about speed. This one has no clock.
- **Equation Balance** is algebra for ages 10 to 16.
