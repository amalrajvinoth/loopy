Chess puzzles on a wooden board. Your child moves the pieces with a tap or a drag. First how each piece moves, then checkmates, winning pieces, defending, clever sacrifices and a good opening. It is in the **Brain games** part of the home page. For ages 5 to 16.

## How to play

1. Read the task above the board, like "White to move. Checkmate in one move!".
2. Tap a piece. Dots show where it can go. A ring shows a piece it can take.
3. Tap where it goes, or drag the piece there.
4. Right: the square turns green and one line says why.
5. Tap **Next** for a new puzzle. **Skip** moves on without an answer.

In a puzzle the other side moves first, so your child sees what just happened. In a longer puzzle, like mate in two, the other side answers each move, and your child plays on. A pawn on the last row asks which piece it becomes.

**Clue** makes the right piece wiggle. Tap it again to see where that piece can go.

On a keyboard, the arrow keys move around the board and **Enter** picks a square.

## When a move is wrong

- The move goes back, and the square turns red.
- A green arrow shows the right move, and one line names it, like "The move: Queen takes on f7."

There is one try per puzzle. Every right answer earns a star. A puzzle that was missed comes back later in **Try again**. See [Try again](/loopy/guide/playing/try-again/).

## Age levels

| Age | Level | What comes up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 to 6 | 1 Meet the pieces | Move a rook, bishop, queen, king, knight or pawn to the star, or take the only piece it can reach |
| 7 to 8 | 2 Checkmates | Mate in one, mate with a pawn, mate in two, castling, en passant, a pawn becomes a queen |
| 9 to 10 | 3 Winning material | Forks, pins, skewers, double attacks, take the defender, a free piece |
| 11 to 12 | 4 Defending | Stop the threat, save a draw, the in-between move, check, mate or stalemate? |
| 13 to 14 | 5 Sacrifice to win | Sacrifices, decoys, clearing a line, attacking the king, mate in three |
| 15 to 16 | 6 Openings and rules | Opening rules, opening traps, board manners |

The levels follow the order a chess school teaches. The level shows next to the score, like "Level 2 of 6". After 10 right in a row, or 18 of the last 20 right, the game goes up a level by itself. After 3 wrong in the last 5 it offers an easier one. See [Levels](/loopy/guide/playing/levels/).

The first time a topic comes up, one line explains it, like "New: Forks. A fork: one knight attacks two pieces at once."

## Where the puzzles come from

Level 1 and "check, mate or stalemate?" are made by the game. The questions about opening rules and board manners were written for loopy. Every other puzzle is a real one, picked from the free Lichess puzzle list, easy ones first. Each one was checked move by move. Nothing is fetched from Lichess while your child plays.

## Settings

Tap ⚙ **Settings** in the game to choose:

- **Level:** one of the six levels.
- **Topic:** **All for my level**, or just one topic, like Forks.
- **Read aloud:** a speaker button reads the task out loud.
- **Print worksheet:** nine boards of your child's level, in black and white, with the letters and numbers round each board. Your child writes the move under the board or draws an arrow. The answers are on page 2.

The level and topic are kept for each age. **Reset score** clears the score and the **Last 5** dots.
