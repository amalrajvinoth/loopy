A sums game for ages 7 to 11 about the six ways to add that German grade 3 teaches. Three ways are for a two-digit number:

- 🪜 **Tens first:** 23 + 20 = 43, then 43 + 9 = 52.
- 🛟 **Nearly round:** 23 + 30 = 53, then 53 − 1 = 52.
- 🧮 **Place by place:** 20 + 20 = 40, 3 + 9 = 12, 40 + 12 = 52.

Three ways are for a one-digit number:

- 👫 **Giants and dwarfs:** 32 + 4 is 2 + 4 = 6, so 36.
- 💞 **To the ten:** 38 + 5 is 38 + 2 = 40, then 40 + 3 = 43.
- 🔟 **Help with 10:** 23 + 9 is 23 + 10 = 33, then 33 − 1 = 32.

The worksheet at school asks five things. Name the way. Pick the way from the sum alone. Say why it is a good one. Tell true from false. Write the steps. The game asks the same five things, one level each.

## How to play

1. The sum sits at the top, like 23 + 29.
2. **Name the way:** the steps are shown. Which way is this? Tap the name. The three names fit the sum: for 23 + 29 they are Tens first, Nearly round and Place by place, for 32 + 4 they are Giants and dwarfs, To the ten and Help with 10.
3. **Pick first:** only the sum is shown. Which way helps here? Each card has the way's picture, its name and a one-line rule, like "the ones go over the ten". One way fits best.
4. **Say why:** the way and its steps are shown. Three reasons come under them. One fits the way, one belongs to another way, one fits nothing. Tap the one that fits.
5. **True or not:** three sentences about the sum, like "29 is 1 or 2 away from a ten, so Nearly round is easy." Only one is true. Tap it.
6. **Fill the steps:** the way is named and the steps have empty boxes. Type each answer and tap **Check**, one box at a time.
7. Tap **Next** for the next sum.

On a keyboard, **1**, **2** and **3** pick an answer, the digits type, **Backspace** erases and **Enter** checks or goes on.

## When an answer is wrong

The child has one try. A wrong answer turns red and the right one green. The line under the sum then says the way with all its steps, like "This is Tens first: 23 + 20 = 43, then 43 + 9 = 52." On the Say why level it adds the reason, on the Fill level the number first: "Not quite. The answer is 43."

A right answer earns a star. A missed question comes back later in **Try again** with the same sum.

## Age levels

| Level | Age | What comes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 7 and under | Name the way from its steps |
| 2 | 8 | Pick first: only the sum, which way helps? |
| 3 | 9 | Say why: the reason that fits the way on show |
| 4 | 10 | True or not: which sentence about the sum is true |
| 5 | 11 and up | Fill the steps: two or three boxes, one after the other |

The sums stay under 100. Half of them add a one-digit number, half a two-digit one, like on the worksheet. A two-digit second number ends in 7, 8 or 9 on Name the way, Say why and Fill the steps, so every way makes sense. On Pick first and True or not the second number is a round ten, nearly round, or has small ones, so exactly one way fits. A one-digit second number always fits one way: 9 or 11 is Help with 10, ones that stay under 10 are Giants and dwarfs, ones that go over the ten are To the ten.

A level saved before Pick first came in keeps its meaning: a child who was at Say why is still at Say why.

After 10 right in a row, or 18 of the last 20 right, the game goes up a level by itself. After 3 wrong in the last 5 it offers an easier one. See [Levels](/loopy/guide/playing/levels/).

## Settings

Tap ⚙ **Settings** in the game to choose:

- **Level:** the five levels above.
- **Read aloud:** a speaker button on the card reads the sum, the steps and the question out loud. It is on by itself for ages 5 to 7.
- **Print worksheet:** 20 sums at the game's level, 4 across. On the first four levels every sum has its three ways to tick and a line for "Because". On the Fill level the way is named and the steps have blanks. Page 2 has the answers: on Pick first and True or not the way that fits, on the other tick sheets one way with its steps as an example, because every way is right.

## Example help text

- "Correct! This is Tens first: 23 + 20 = 43, then 43 + 9 = 52."
- "Correct! 8 + 5 goes over the ten, so To the ten helps. To the ten: 38 + 2 = 40, then 40 + 3 = 43."
- "Not quite. Nearly round: 27 + 50 = 77, then 77 − 1 = 76. 49 is nearly 50, so I add 50 and take 1 away."
- "Correct! 22 is 1 or 2 away from a ten, so Nearly round is easy. Nearly round: 48 + 20 = 68, then 68 + 2 = 70."
- "Not quite. The answer is 14. Place by place: 50 + 10 = 60, 5 + 9 = 14, 60 + 14 = 74."
