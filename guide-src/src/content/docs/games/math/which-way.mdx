A sums game for ages 7 to 10 about the three ways to add two numbers that German grade 3 teaches: **Tens first** (23 + 20 = 43, then 43 + 9 = 52), **Nearly round** (23 + 30 = 53, then 53 − 1 = 52) and **Place by place** (20 + 20 = 40, 3 + 9 = 12, 40 + 12 = 52). The test at school asks the child to name a way, to say why it is a good one, to tell true from false, and to write the steps. The game asks the same four things, one level each.

## How to play

1. The sum sits at the top, like 23 + 29.
2. **Name the way:** the steps are shown. Which way is this? Tap **Tens first**, **Nearly round** or **Place by place**.
3. **Say why:** the way and its steps are shown. Three reasons come under them. One fits the way, one belongs to another way, one fits nothing. Tap the one that fits.
4. **True or not:** three sentences about the sum, like "29 is 1 or 2 away from a ten, so Nearly round is easy." Only one is true. Tap it.
5. **Fill the steps:** the way is named and the steps have empty boxes. Type each answer and tap **Check**, one box at a time.
6. Tap **Next** for the next sum.

On a keyboard, **1**, **2** and **3** pick an answer, the digits type, **Backspace** erases and **Enter** checks or goes on.

## When an answer is wrong

The child has one try. A wrong answer turns red and the right one green. The line under the sum then says the way with all its steps, like "This is Tens first: 23 + 20 = 43, then 43 + 9 = 52." On the Say why level it adds the reason, on the Fill level the number first: "Not quite. The answer is 43."

A right answer earns a star. A missed question comes back later in **Try again** with the same sum.

## Age levels

| Level | Age | What comes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 7 and under | Name the way from its steps |
| 2 | 8 | Say why: the reason that fits the way on show |
| 3 | 9 | True or not: which sentence about the sum is true |
| 4 | 10 and up | Fill the steps: two or three boxes, one after the other |

The sums stay under 100. Both numbers have tens and ones, and the second one ends in 7, 8 or 9 on the first, second and fourth levels, so every way makes sense. On the True or not level the second number is a round ten, nearly round, or has small ones, so exactly one sentence is true.

After 10 right in a row, or 18 of the last 20 right, the game goes up a level by itself. After 3 wrong in the last 5 it offers an easier one. See [Levels](/loopy/guide/playing/levels/).

## Settings

Tap ⚙ **Settings** in the game to choose:

- **Level:** the four levels above.
- **Read aloud:** a speaker button on the card reads the sum, the steps and the question out loud. It is on by itself for ages 5 to 7.
- **Print worksheet:** 20 sums at the game's level, 4 across. On the first three levels every sum has the three ways to tick and a line for "Because". On the Fill level the way is named and the steps have blanks. Page 2 has the answers: on the tick sheets one way with its steps as an example, because every way is right.

## Example help text

- "Correct! This is Tens first: 23 + 20 = 43, then 43 + 9 = 52."
- "Not quite. Nearly round: 27 + 50 = 77, then 77 − 1 = 76. 49 is nearly 50, so I add 50 and take 1 away."
- "Correct! 22 is 1 or 2 away from a ten, so Nearly round is easy. Nearly round: 48 + 20 = 68, then 68 + 2 = 70."
- "Not quite. The answer is 14. Place by place: 50 + 10 = 60, 5 + 9 = 14, 60 + 14 = 74."

The ways with their own practice are in [Step by Step](/loopy/guide/games/math/step-by-step/), where the child works a sum one step at a time.
