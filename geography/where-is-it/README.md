# Where Is It?

A map game. The game names a place and the child taps it on the map: a continent or an ocean, a country, or the country that has a given capital. Europe first, then the whole world. In German it is called "Wo liegt das?".

## How to play

1. Read the question at the top, like "Where is France?" or "Paris is the capital of which country?".
2. Tap the place on the map. The right place turns green.
3. If the tap was wrong, that place turns red, and the map shows both names.
4. A short line under the map says what the place is, and its capital.
5. Tap **Next** to go on. Every right answer earns a star.

Small countries, like Luxembourg, are hard to hit with a finger. A tap just next to a small country counts as a tap on it.

## Age levels

| Age | What the game starts with |
| --- | --- |
| 7 to 8 | Continents and oceans on a world map |
| 9 | Well-known countries of Europe |
| 10 | All countries of Europe |
| 11 | Capitals of Europe |
| 12 | Well-known countries around the world |
| 13 and up | All countries and capitals around the world |

"Well-known" means the big or famous countries, like France, Italy or Brazil. Younger children only get those, so the map is not too hard.

In World mode the map shows one part of the world at a time (Europe, Africa, Asia, North America, South America, Australia and Oceania), so every country is big enough to tap. A small tag under the question says which part, like "In Africa".

The level shows next to the score, like "Level 2 of 5". After 10 right in a row the game offers the next level. After 3 wrong in the last 5 it offers an easier one, once a visit. A level picked by hand in Settings is kept for that age. The levels: continents and oceans, countries of Europe, capitals of Europe, countries of the world, capitals of the world.

## Last 5

At the top, **Last 5** shows your last 5 answers as dots: green for right, red for wrong. The newest one is on the right.
They stay there next time you play. **Reset score** in Settings clears them.

## Settings

Open them with the gear button.

- **Map.** Continents (continents and oceans), Europe or World.
- **Find.** Countries or Capitals. Not used for Continents.
- The map and mode are remembered for each age, so a younger brother or sister keeps their own level.
- **Read aloud.** A speaker button reads the question out loud.
- Age, colors, sound effects and the language (EN/DE) are shared with the other games.
- **Print worksheet.** See below.

## Print a worksheet

Tap **Settings**, then **Print worksheet**. The print window opens. Print it, or save it as a PDF.

- **Continents and Countries:** page 1 has 20 small maps. One place on each is grey. The child writes its name on the line.
- **Capitals:** page 1 has 20 countries. The child writes each capital on the line.
- Very small countries are left out of the map sheets, so every grey place is easy to see.
- Page 2 has the answers, for example "France" or "France: Paris".

## Example help text

- "Where is Italy?" / "Wo liegt Italien?"
- "This is Italy. Its capital is Rome." / "Das ist Italien. Hauptstadt: Rom."
- "That was Spain. Shown in green: Portugal." / "Das war Spanien. Grün markiert: Portugal."
- "Bern is the capital of which country?" / "Bern ist die Hauptstadt von welchem Land?"

## Places

- 7 continents and 5 oceans.
- 39 countries in Europe and 125 more around the world, each with its English and German name.
- Capitals in English and German (Rome and Rom, Warsaw and Warschau). A few countries have more than one capital, or one that people disagree about. The capital questions skip those: Bolivia, Israel, South Africa, Sri Lanka and Eswatini.
- Some places are on the map but never asked, like Greenland, Western Sahara, Kosovo, Taiwan and Palestine.

## For grown-ups

- The maps are simple outlines drawn in the page itself. Nothing is loaded from other websites.
- The outlines come from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) (public domain), at the 1:110 million scale. Borders are simplified, so they are close but not exact.
- The map uses the Mercator projection, like most wall maps and map apps. Places far north and south, like Greenland and Russia, look bigger than they are.
- The places, names and capitals are in the `PLACES` list in `index.html`. Each one has a map view and a level: 1 for well-known, 2 for the rest.
- The progress page groups missed places by mode, like "Practise the countries of Europe on a map."
